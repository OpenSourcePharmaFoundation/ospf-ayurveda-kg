## KEY AGREEMENTS (≥4 agents agree)

1. **UDCA is the strongest non-benchmark development candidate** — ranked #1 non-benchmark by Chemist, Clinical Landscape, and in the top 3 by Target Profiler and Disease Modeler. Tight score range (6.0-7.5) indicates consistent moderate-high assessment.

2. **Fidaxomicin is the undisputed benchmark** — highest or near-highest by every agent. Mean 8.9.

3. **Conessine should be deprioritized as a CANDIDATE** — bottom 2 by all 5 agents. The Chemist largely falsified the CspC scaffold hypothesis on structural grounds. However, Target Profiler notes the TARGET (#7 CspC) is arguably the most valuable in the model.

4. **Narrow-spectrum antibiotics are a burned value proposition** — all agents flag ibezapolstat's strategic problem despite decent individual scores.

5. **Non-absorption is the dominant positive predictor for CDI** — flagged by all 5 agents independently.

## KEY CONFLICTS (agents disagree by ≥3 points)

1. **Ebselen: Target Profiler (8.5) vs ADMET (4.0)** — 4.5-point spread
   - Target Profiler: Best target portfolio (Tier 1 CPD + Tier 2 PrdB); ideal covalent cysteine chemotype
   - ADMET: Se electrophile quenched by luminal thiols; reaches neither lumen effectively (consumed) nor host cells (absorbed too little)
   - Chemist agrees with ADMET (5.0): "most over-rated candidate on chemistry grounds"
   - Disease Modeler sides with Target (8.0): three-node mechanism, functionally an oral bezlotoxumab
   - **RESOLUTION NEEDED: Is ebselen's warhead viable in the colonic environment?**

2. **Vancomycin: ADMET (9.0) vs Disease Modeler (4.0)** — 5.0-point spread
   - ADMET: Ideal luminal PK, zero absorption, massive fecal concentration headroom
   - Disease Modeler: Worsens Phase 0 and Module C; deepens the recurrence loop
   - **RESOLUTION: Both are correct — vancomycin has ideal PK and terrible disease biology. This reflects the disease's central paradox.**

3. **Niclosamide: Disease Modeler (7.5) vs ADMET (3.0)** — 4.5-point spread
   - Disease Modeler: Addresses Module A (TcdB entry blockade); broad phase coverage
   - ADMET: Nitro reduced anaerobically → pharmacophore destroyed; solubility-capped
   - Chemist agrees with ADMET (4.0): mechanism mislabelled; protonophore is non-selective
   - **RESOLUTION NEEDED: Fecal slurry redox stability test is decisive.**

4. **Berberine: ADMET (8.0) vs Chemist (5.0)** — 3.0-point spread
   - ADMET: Best intrinsic luminal profile among repurposables (permanent cation, <1% F)
   - Chemist: Permanent cation = severe fecal binding; iminium reduction to absorbable dihydroberberine; sub-MIC toxin induction risk
   - **RESOLUTION NEEDED: Fecal-matrix MIC and redox stability data are decisive.**

## GAPS IDENTIFIED FOR PHASE 2

1. **No agent restores colonization resistance** (Disease Modeler's loudest concern)
2. **Phase 1 (germination) has no credible candidate** after conessine downgrade
3. **Phase 3 (toxin gene regulation) is essentially empty** — no candidate targets TcdR/CodY/CcpA
4. **Phase 4 repair is unaddressed** — no Wnt/EGFR/GLP-2R candidates
5. **Ebselen viability in reducing colonic environment** — central unresolved question
6. **UDCA colonic delivery mechanism** — needs resolution (parent vs LCA downstream)
7. **Berberine fecal binding and sub-MIC toxin induction** — experimental data needed
